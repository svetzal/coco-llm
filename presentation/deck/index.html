Things that never existed

EXP-012 · the fake episode titles

One keystroke, sixteen invented titles. The lesson is the split and it is visible in the sizes: 400 bytes of model holding the shape of a title, 1,600 bytes of dictionary and rules holding the words. It never learned what a Gothos is. It learned that something goes there. Corpus number reframes it: across all 79 real titles, only two adjacent word pairs ever repeat. Nothing to generalize from. It memorized a shape. FIRST TO CUT. Also the one place we leave the vintage-computer example, so get in and out.

EXP-003 · the fan-corpus bias runs

Same architecture, same initial weights, same budget, same vocabulary, same sampling seed. Different data. Different machine. Then the harder half: the same balanced examples concatenated and interleaved produce different behaviour. Order is a choice too, and nobody set out to make it one. Now you play it

EXP-013 · the game opponent that learns

NEVER CUT. Someone from the room calls throws. It does not know the rules: RULES 0/25 says so. It does not know the player either: MEMORY n/rounds says how little it has. Both stay on screen. Six or eight rounds. It states its expectation BEFORE each throw. Around twenty-five rounds it has the rules; it never finishes learning the person. Press R in front of them and let it fall over. QUOTE THE HUMAN NUMBER: 80% against six synthetic players with habits. 52.8% against a recorded 200-round session with a person who was trying. Better than chance at three sigma, nowhere near enough to win. Five of six synthetic players had a habit and one did not. A person plays like the one that did not. The test set encoded an assumption about people and the average hid it. 100 bytes. No neural network. A tiny table beat every model we tried, which is why there is no model in there at all. Invite whoever played to the table.
