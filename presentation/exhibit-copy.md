## Open question before anything is printed

The abstract says the CoCo is "from 1981." The bio says it arrived at Christmas
45 years ago, which lands on Christmas 1980. Both may be defensible depending on
whether the date refers to the gift or to manufacture, but the two texts will be
read side by side. Settle this before print and make both agree.

## Talk abstract

**CoCo-LLM: Definitely not large, barely even a language model, and the coolest
thing I've done yet on my CoCo**

Watch my Tandy Colour Computer from 1981 start from random numbers, make up
computer names that never existed, and then start producing names — and 1980s
marketing phrases — that sound completely real. Just like with modern "LLMs"
there is really no difference, to the machine, between nonsense and wonder. The
wonder we see is not the machine, not the math, not even the software, it's the
people that invented what it was trained on.

Join me and let's demystify "AI" and "LLMs" by following the journey I took this
summer to build a 6809 assembly-language training and inference system on my
CoCo. As we look under the hood we'll play with the training data, and try some
nifty things we can do with these techniques.

## Bio

I got this computer for Christmas 45 years ago. It's fair to say it's
responsible for my entire career, and a good part of who I am. I've written code
every day since.

I love art, music, and technology — and helping people understand how to
leverage it. I live in a log cabin in the woods in the Haliburton Highlands. At
RBC I build agentic AI automation and help people figure out what the hell to do
with all these new tools that keep showing up.

I make mistakes for a living. I say that out loud because mistakes are how we
learn, and how we find success. I made plenty building this one — ask me about
them.

## Copy discipline

The rules that govern the slides govern the print:

- No runtime claim until it has been measured on the physical CoCo. The ~75
  second figure in the README is a cycle-model projection and is not printable.
- No sample output unless it came from a real run. Names such as
  `COMMODORE COLOR ST` are illustrative in `learning-journey.md` and must be
  labelled as such or omitted.
- The model invents *plausible* names. Do not print a claim that it produces
  real ones — that claim is the thing the talk exists to dismantle.
